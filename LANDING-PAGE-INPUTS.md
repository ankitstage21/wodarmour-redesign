## B. Problems with what's already there

**1. The reviews are a liability as often as an asset.**
178 at 4.60 is a strong number. The content underneath it is not:
- Three of the five visible reviews have the body text `7841635205336` — an order or phone number, not a review.
- One reads "I bought these February 2023" — an imported US review, not an Indian buyer.
- A prominent **4★ says the buyer returned it and paid extra for the Elites because "the hose is obnoxious."**
- Six 1★ reviews, unexplained.

The rating badge helps. The review text, as displayed, actively costs sales. This needs curation before it goes on a landing page.

**2. Two different prices on one page.** The variant is ₹99,980. The description and the FAQ both say ₹99,890. Whichever is right, a buyer who notices reads it as sloppiness on a ₹1L purchase.

**3. Preorder is framed as a caveat, not an offer.** "Ships as soon as possible (expected 30th August 2026)" reads like an apology. It's nine days out — that's a *reserve your unit* moment, not a warning.

**4. The strongest trust asset is a grey bar of small text.** "GST Invoice Available, Free Shipping Pan India, 2 Years warranty" is the authorised-dealer moat, rendered as a throwaway line.

## C. Missing — and each one is a live objection

Ranked by how much I think they cost in conversion.

**1. EMI, specifically.** At ₹99,980 this is often *the* deciding factor in India. Needed: is it genuinely no-cost, which banks, how many months, minimum card limit, and what the monthly figure actually is. "Easy installment options" converts nobody.

**2. Returns / trial.** The unspoken question at ₹1L is "what if it doesn't suit me?" Needed: window, who pays return shipping, restocking fee, condition requirements. Silence here reads as *no returns*.

**3. Fit and sizing.** "Universal fit sleeves" is not enough. Normatec sells standard and tall internationally. A 6'3" buyer will not risk ₹1L on a guess. Needed: inseam/thigh circumference range, and whether tall is available.

**4. What's in the box.** Not stated anywhere. Control unit, two leg attachments, hoses, adapter, bag? At this price people want the manifest before they commit.

**5. Comparison against the obvious alternatives.** This is the single biggest lever on a considered purchase. Buyers *will* compare — the only question is whether they do it on your page or someone else's. Needed: Normatec 3 vs **Elite** (wireless, ₹1,28,990) vs **Go** (₹49,990) vs **JetBoots Prime** (₹54,999) vs cheap marketplace boots.
   Note their own 4★ review is exactly this comparison, made by a customer who left. Answer it honestly — "the 3 is wired and costs ₹29k less; the Elite is wireless" — and you keep people the hose objection currently loses.

**6. Warranty detail.** Two years covering what? Pump and sleeves both? How is a claim made, where is service performed, what's the turnaround? "2-year warranty" is a headline; buyers at this price read the terms.

**7. Real Indian proof.** Named buyers, cities, sport, ideally photos. One credible review from a Bengaluru Hyrox athlete outperforms 100 imported ones.

**8. Video of it actually running.** 15–30 seconds of the chambers inflating in sequence on a real leg. More convincing than any diagram — including the one I built.

**9. Contraindications.** Compression therapy has real ones: DVT, severe peripheral arterial disease, open wounds, pregnancy. This is a medical-adjacent device. Omitting them is both a trust gap and a liability.

**10. Delivery date by pincode.** "2–6 working days" is generic. "Delivered to 560001 by Tuesday" converts.

**11. Business / clinic purchase.** GST invoice is mentioned but not sold. Gyms, physios and clinics can claim input tax credit — that's a real argument, and a bulk enquiry path is worth having.

**12. Evidence of authorised status.** The claim is the moat. A dealer certificate, or a link to Hyperice's own partner listing, turns an assertion into proof.
